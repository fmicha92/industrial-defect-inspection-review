# Collaborative Discrepancy Optimization for Reliable Image Anomaly Localization

## Summary

- The paper proposes Collaborative Discrepancy Optimization (CDO) for unsupervised image anomaly localization.
- CDO targets overgeneralization in feature-discrepancy methods by optimizing normal and synthetic abnormal feature discrepancy distributions together.
- The method is evaluated on MVTec2D, MVTec3D, and a real-world automotive plastic parts inspection dataset.

## Contribution

- Claimed: CDO improves prediction reliability by increasing the margin and reducing the overlap between normal and abnormal discrepancy distributions.
- Shown: On MVTec2D, CDO reports 1.11% AU-ROC and 2.26% AU-PRO gains over ST, and 2.57% AU-PRO over PaDiM. On MVTec3D, it reports a 10.72% AU-PRO gain over 3D-ST. On automotive plastic parts, CDO with WRes50 reports 90.30 AU-PRO versus 82.60 for ST.
- Inferred: Synthetic perturbation quality matters because the authors name better anomaly generation, such as GANs, as future work.

## Method

- Expert-apprentice feature discrepancy setup.
- Margin Optimization Module minimizes normal discrepancies and maximizes synthetic abnormal discrepancies.
- Overlap Optimization Module dynamically weights hard normal and abnormal samples to reduce distribution overlap.
- Synthetic abnormal inputs are generated by random perturbation because real anomalies are unavailable during training.

## Limitations

- Real anomalies are not used in training; synthetic perturbations stand in for abnormal samples.
- The authors suggest GAN-based anomaly generation as future work, implying current random perturbations may be limited.
- Dataset and annotation details for the automotive plastic parts data are only summarized in the extracted source.
